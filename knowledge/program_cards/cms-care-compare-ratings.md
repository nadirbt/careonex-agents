## Summary

Care Compare (on Medicare.gov) shows **two separate** star ratings for home
health agencies, and families often don't realize they measure different
things. Explaining that distinction — and that ratings lag real time — is
more useful than reciting a specific agency's stars, which this card does
not contain (see "What's deliberately not a card" in the knowledge README;
agency-specific ratings should come from a live lookup, not this card).

## What the two ratings measure

- **Quality of Patient Care Star Rating** — based on clinical data (OASIS
  assessments + Medicare claims), covering 7 measures: timely initiation of
  care, and improvement in oral medication management, ambulation, bed
  transferring, bathing, dyspnea (breathing), plus a "potentially
  preventable hospitalization" measure. An agency needs data on at least 5
  of 7 measures to get a rating.
- **Patient Survey (HHCAHPS) Star Rating** — based on patient experience
  surveys: communication, professionalism, overall experience. This is a
  *different* survey-based score, not a clinical outcomes score.

Both are **relative** ratings: an agency is scored against how it compares
to other agencies nationally, not against a fixed target. Most agencies
land at 3 stars ("about average"); above 3 means better than average
performance on the measured items, below 3 means worse.

## Why it matters on the call

- Ratings update **quarterly** — "the agency says so" is the right instinct
  from the SOW: if asked for a rating, note the data lags real time, don't
  imply it's live.
- A high star rating is not a guarantee of fit — it reflects aggregate
  performance on specific measures, not whether an agency has availability,
  serves the caller's language, or fits the specific care plan.
- 3 stars does not mean "bad" — it means average, which the source data
  explicitly states is "delivering good quality of care" for most agencies.

## What to say on the call

"Medicare's Care Compare site gives home health agencies a star rating —
but it's actually two separate ratings, one for clinical outcomes and one
for patient experience, and they update every few months, not in real
time. Three stars is about average, not a red flag. I can help you look up
a specific agency if you have one in mind."
