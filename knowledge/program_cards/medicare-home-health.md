## Summary

This is the single most common misunderstanding families have on the call:
**Medicare does not pay for ongoing custodial help** (bathing, dressing,
cleaning) as a standalone service. It only pays for home health when there
is also a skilled medical need and the person is "homebound." Correcting
this belief gently, early, is one of the highest-value things this card
does.

## Who qualifies

Both of these must be true (the "homebound" test):

- Leaving home isn't recommended because of a condition, or requires help
  (cane, wheelchair, walker, special transport, another person) because of
  illness or injury.
- Normally unable to leave home, and leaving takes considerable effort.
  (Short, infrequent absences — medical appointments, religious services,
  adult day care — don't disqualify someone.)

And the person needs **part-time or intermittent skilled care**: skilled
nursing, physical/occupational therapy, or speech-language pathology. A
doctor or NP must see the person, confirm the need, and order the care from
a Medicare-certified home health agency.

## What it pays for

- Skilled nursing (wound care, injections, patient/caregiver education,
  monitoring unstable conditions), physical/occupational therapy,
  speech-language pathology, medical social services.
- Home health **aide** care — but *only* alongside one of the skilled
  services above, not by itself.
- Durable medical equipment and medical supplies used at home.

**Medicare does NOT pay for:** 24-hour care, home meal delivery, homemaker
services (shopping, cleaning) unrelated to a care plan, or custodial/personal
care (bathing, dressing, toileting help) when that is the *only* care
needed. This is the gap that Medicaid MLTSS, JACC, or private pay usually
fill instead — worth naming those alternatives in the same breath.

## How to apply

A doctor or health care provider must see the person in person, confirm the
need, and order care through a Medicare-certified home health agency. The
provider should give a list of agencies serving the area, and must disclose
any financial relationship with an agency they recommend.

## Key numbers

- Cost: $0 for covered home health services (after meeting the Part B
  deductible, 20% coinsurance applies to durable medical equipment).
- Typical visit cap: up to 8 hours/day combined nursing + aide care, max 28
  hours/week ("part-time or intermittent"); short-term exceptions up to 35
  hours/week possible if a provider deems it necessary.
- No limit on total number of home health visits if the person continues to
  qualify.

## What to say on the call

"A lot of families assume Medicare pays for someone to help around the
house — it actually only covers home health when there's a skilled medical
need, like nursing or therapy, and the person mostly can't leave home. If
what you need is more day-to-day help with bathing or dressing, that
usually comes from Medicaid, a state program, or private pay instead."
