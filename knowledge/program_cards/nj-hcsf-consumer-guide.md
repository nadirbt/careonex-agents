## Summary

This is the card that answers "how do I know an agency is legitimate?" In
New Jersey, any agency that places or arranges personnel to provide home
care, companion care, or personal care in someone's residence must be
registered with the state as a **Health Care Service Firm (HCSF)**. An
agency that isn't registered is operating outside the rules meant to protect
families.

## Who this applies to

Any business that places or arranges placement of personnel to provide
health care, companion care, or personal care services in the personal
residence of someone with a disability or age 60+. This includes agencies
employing Certified Homemaker-Home Health Aides (CHHAs, certified by the NJ
Board of Nursing).

## What registration requires (so a family knows what "legitimate" means)

To register or renew, a firm must have:

- A $500 application fee (initial) and post a **$10,000 bond** (unless
  waived by the Division Director).
- A designated health care practitioner supervisor — a NJ-licensed
  registered nurse or physician.
- Accreditation from a body recognized by the NJ Dept. of Human Services,
  if the firm participates in Medicaid Personal Care Assistance.
- An annual financial statement consistent with the firm's state tax filing.
- As of the 2026 renewal cycle, HCSF registrations expire **September 30**
  each year (changed from the prior June 30 deadline) — a firm operating on
  an expired registration is a warning sign.

## How to check if an agency is registered

Families can verify a specific agency through the NJ Division of Consumer
Affairs' online **Regulated Business Online Registration Portal** (linked
from njconsumeraffairs.gov/hcservice), which lists license/registration
numbers, names, addresses, and status. Separately, the **NJ Department of
Health** licenses "Home Health Agencies" (a different, overlapping
category, relevant for Medicare-certified home health) — that list is
available via data.nj.gov and NJ DOH's facility search tool.

## What to say on the call

"Before you sign anything, it's worth checking that an agency is registered
with the state as a Health Care Service Firm — that's what requires them to
have a bonded, insured setup and a nurse or doctor supervising care. I can
send you the link to look an agency up, or check one for you right now if
you have a name."
