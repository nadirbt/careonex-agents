## Summary

This program pays for a **break for the caregiver**, not ongoing care for
the person receiving care. If a family's real problem is caregiver burnout —
someone caring for a parent daily without pay — this is the program to
mention, distinct from JACC or MLTSS which fund care itself.

## Who qualifies

The person *receiving* care must:

- Be 18 or older, an NJ resident, living in the community (not a facility).
- Receive daily basic care and/or supervision from an **uncompensated**
  caregiver (spouse, family, friend) who is 18 or older.
- Have functional impairments requiring another person's help, certified by
  a licensed medical provider.
- Meet income/asset guidelines (2026 figures found: roughly $2,982/mo
  individual or $5,964/mo couple gross income; $40,000/$60,000 in assets —
  treat as indicative pending verification).
- **Not** currently enrolled in MLTSS, JACC, Alzheimer's Adult Day Services,
  or Congregate Housing Services Program. Switching *from* JACC or
  Alzheimer's Adult Day *into* Statewide Respite is allowed.

## What it pays for

Short-term or periodic relief for the caregiver: in-home respite care,
companionship, or adult day care attendance, on a sliding scale from 0% to
25% of the cost of services based on income.

## How to apply

Contact the county ADRC / Area Agency on Aging: **1-877-222-3737**.

## Key numbers

- Sliding-scale co-pay: 0%–25% of service cost, based on income.
- Income/asset figures above are approximate — confirm current year's
  guidelines with the ADRC before quoting.

## What to say on the call

"If the bigger issue is that whoever's caring for your parent day-to-day
needs a break, there's a respite program that pays for someone to step in
temporarily, on a sliding scale based on income. Want me to connect you to
the county aging office to check eligibility?"
